# Gameplay Actions, Controls, Resources, and UI

## Purpose and acceptance boundary

This document is the action-and-control portion of the official Universal Paperclips gameplay inventory. It records observable game behavior, not a JavaScript porting plan. The companion [product requirements](product.md) define the server-authoritative clone and fidelity boundary. Project details belong to [Gameplay Projects](gameplay-projects.md), full stage/ending/prestige behavior to [Gameplay Progression](gameplay-progression.md) and [Gameplay Combat and Endgame](gameplay-combat-endgame.md).

Source locators below use the official capture's logical file names and 1-based lines. The official reference snapshot is the single owner of public source URLs and capture hashes. No local capture path is part of this requirement.

The official page contains **61 active inline HTML click handlers**, in source order `HTML-001` through `HTML-061`. They call **51 distinct targets**: **48 implemented functions** and the three undefined legacy names `feedSwarm`, `teachSwarm`, and `cladSwarm`. Each of the 48 implemented targets has exactly one required `ACT-*` feature below. Four functions have multiple HTML argument variants, so 58 active handlers bind to those 48 features. The other three handlers bind only to exclusions. The complete handler-level mapping is in [the machine-readable inventory index](./data/gameplay-inventory.json).

Normal behavior means behavior available through the official, appropriately enabled control. Direct console calls, negative arguments, partial batch calls that bypass the button gate, and mutation of internal variables are not player mechanics. In the clone, normal costs and preconditions are authoritative game rules; a client-side disabled state alone is not sufficient validation.

Meaningful costs, resource changes, strategic formulas, unlocks, status feedback, and stage behavior are required. Exact browser timer frequencies, tab throttling, floating-point tails, storage schemas, first-paint CSS, DOM structure, formatting helpers, and known disabled-guard/equality discrepancies are not independent product requirements. They are noted only where needed to distinguish normal behavior from console-only artifacts.

## 3. Automatic systems coupled to actions

### `SYS-DEMAND-SALES` - pricing, demand, sales, and revenue

- In Human play, calculate demand as `(.8 / margin) * 1.1^(marketingLvl-1) * marketingEffectiveness * demandBoost`, then multiply by `(1 + prestigeU/10)`. Public Demand displays `demand * 10` percent.
- At each of the source-normalized 10 sales opportunities per second, an independent random roll succeeds with probability `demand/100` (capped in effect at certainty for demand >= 100). Exact browser scheduling is not required; this normalized opportunity rate is gameplay-relevant. Success requests `floor(.7 * demand^1.15)` clips, limited by Unsold Inventory. Stockouts sell nothing.
- A sale decreases only Unsold Inventory, adds Funds, and updates sales/income accounting; it does not reduce lifetime or Unused Clips. Demand and batch size are deterministic for a given state, while transaction occurrence is stochastic.
- Show Funds, Unsold Inventory, Price, Public Demand, and after RevTracker average clips sold/revenue. Average revenue uses analytic demand when stock is sufficient and rolling realized revenue when stock-limited; it is not interchangeable with the raw sales roll.
- Sources: `main.js:3073-3089,3163,3227-3264,4308-4317,4564-4587`; `index2.html:261-287`.

### `SYS-WIRE-MARKET` - repricing and automatic purchase

- Direct purchases are deterministic. At each source-normalized 100 ms slow-loop opportunity, an independent 1.5% repricing chance advances the oscillation counter and sets price to `ceil(wireBasePrice + 6*sin(counter))` (`SYS-WIRE-MARKET-RATES`, precision within this existing system, not a separate feature).
- Each purchase raises that base by $0.05 and resets inactivity. After more than 250 slow opportunities without a purchase, a base above $15 is multiplied by 0.999 and the inactivity timer resets. Repricing remains stochastic; there is no requirement to reproduce the original random sequence.
- WireBuyer uses the ordinary purchase path at Wire <= 1 and never bypasses Funds affordability. Show current stock/cost and ON/OFF state; no separate automatic discount exists.
- Sources: `main.js:695-732,4239-4243`; `globals.js:14-16,39-40,104-105,143-144`.

### `SYS-CLIP-PRODUCTION` - shared conversion and matter chain

- Manual clicks, AutoClippers, MegaClippers, and Factories all use the same Wire-limited clip conversion and update lifetime, Unsold, and Unused ledgers. Baseline AutoClipper/MegaClipper output is 1/500 clips/s per machine before boosts; factory output uses its base rate, Power performance, and factory upgrades.
- Harvesters convert Available Matter -> Acquired Matter; Wire Drones convert Acquired Matter -> Wire; clip production converts Wire -> Paperclips. Every conversion is capped by its input. Drone work scales with Power, upgrades, and Work/Think.
- When factory/drone boosts activate, the captured formula introduces an additional asset-level factor rather than merely applying a constant multiplier. This materially changes late-game scaling and must not be simplified away.

### `SYS-TRUST-OPS-CREATIVITY` - computational capacity

### `SYS-QUANTUM-WAVES` - chip state behind Compute

### `SYS-QUANTUM-OVERFLOW-DAMPING` - raw qOps versus credited Operations

- Let `Q = ceil(sum(chip values) * 360)`, `B = memory*1000 - standardOps`, and `D = tempOps/100 + 5`. If `Q > B`, add `ceil(Q/D) - B` to temporary Ops and add B to standard Ops, filling normal free capacity. Otherwise credit Q to standard Ops normally.
- The immediate combined positive credit is therefore based on `ceil(Q/D)`, not raw Q. Existing temporary Ops increase D, creating diminishing returns on repeated overflow clicks. Negative-qOps behavior is not transformed into another resource.
- The click display reports uncompressed raw Q as "qOps" even when credited Operations are lower. This information mismatch is part of the observed feedback and must not be silently replaced with unrestricted raw overflow.
- Overflow resets the temporary-op fade delay; after that delay, fade increases and deducts temporary Ops, which can merge into spare standard capacity. Exact timer cadence and floating-point residue remain implementation details.
- Sources: `main.js:837-864,3413-3450`; `globals.js:149-154`.

### `SYS-INVESTMENT-AUTO` - portfolio automation

- Deposit/Withdraw are deterministic cash transfers. Automatic buying later selects stochastic symbols and price bands subject to Cash, risk-dependent reserves, and a maximum of five holdings. Quantities are integer shares.
- Eligible Human-stage purchase checks at 1 Hz buy with 25% probability. Initial price-band probabilities are 1%/14%/25%/40%/20% with caps 3000/500/150/50/15 before the existing budget adjustment. Every 2.5-second update gives each holding a 60% move chance with step `ceil(U*price/(4*riskiness))`, where U is uniform on [0,1). After `sellDelay >= 5`, each eligible check has a 30% chance to sell the oldest holding. These are `SYS-INVESTMENT-DISTRIBUTIONS`, precision within this existing system rather than a new feature. Engine upgrades improve gain tendency; risk changes reserves and step size. There is no player-facing individual-stock buy/sell button.
- Show Cash, Stocks, Total, up to five symbol/amount/price/total/P-L rows, engine level/cost, and periodic lifetime investment reports. Portfolio and price activity stop outside Human play.

This document owns their direct action gates and resource/interface connections.
