# Gameplay Projects

## 1. Scope and status

This document specifies the player-facing behavior of every entry in the official `projects` array. It is a source-grounded gameplay inventory for requirements and verification, not a delivery plan. It does not allocate work to milestones, define M1, or propose an implementation grouping.

- **Coverage:** 96 projects, all 96 `projects.push(...)` entries, and 96 unique official variable IDs. Every entry is in scope, including restart, prestige, alternate-ending, and disassembly paths. No project is excluded as unused.
- **Lifecycle totals:** 90 are one-shot in ordinary uninterrupted play; 6 are repeatable: **PRJ-002, PRJ-044, PRJ-047, PRJ-073, PRJ-075, and PRJ-096**. PRJ-095's reload behavior is a page-lifecycle caveat, not a seventh repeatable project.
- **Canonical order:** PRJ-001 through PRJ-096 follow actual array insertion order. This is not numeric-suffix order: for example, `project17` precedes `project16`, and `project34`, `project70`, and `project35` precede `project27`. This order is a source binding, not a proposed delivery sequence.
- **Official identity:** each row preserves the official variable ID, displayed name, cost, visibility predicate, direct effect, lifecycle, dependencies, and source locator. DOM IDs are not implementation requirements; where needed, the official DOM button ID is `projectButton` plus the variable suffix, including `projectButton10b` and `projectButton40b`.
- **Fidelity level:** preserve gameplay-significant rules, values, formulas, dependencies, information, and feedback. Do not reproduce JavaScript organization, DOM mechanics, browser storage formats, incidental floating-point tails, or exact random sequences.

### Notation and dependency terms

- `ops` means Operations, `Creativity` means the Creativity currency, `MEM` means Memory, and `MW-seconds` means stored power. Yomi, Trust, Honor, liquid funds, total clips, and unused clips are distinct resources.
- Unless a row explicitly says total or historical clips, a clip price consumes **unused clips**. A dollar price consumes liquid funds rather than portfolio valuation.
- **Visibility trigger** controls offer eligibility. It does not auto-purchase the project. Cost affordability is a separate predicate, even when a resource threshold appears in both.
- **Hard prerequisite** means a predicate or completion flag required by the source. **Practical prerequisite** means a system normally needed to reach the predicate, pay the cost, or make the effect useful; its absence from the explicit trigger matters.
- `PRJ-nnn complete` refers to that project's persistent completion state, not merely an offer being visible.
- All direct project purchases are deterministic given current state. Dynamic prices and titles are deterministic functions of the state identified in their rows.

## 3. Project display, offers, and lifecycle

### 3.1 Global project display and unlock

The Projects panel is hidden until `projectsFlag=1`. The official progression logic enables Trust-Constrained Self-Modification either during the early resource crisis (no unsold clips, insufficient liquid funds for wire, and no wire) or at 2,000 total clips (`main.js:3455-3483`; panel display at `main.js:1253-1258`; container at `index2.html:469-478`). Individual project predicates can be evaluated independently of whether the panel is currently visible. PRJ-039 explicitly uses `projectsFlag=1`; most other projects use their own state gates.

A faithful implementation must preserve the difference between the global panel unlock and each project's own offer trigger. It need not reproduce DOM hiding as its authority mechanism.

### 3.2 Offer eligibility and purchase

The official offer manager scans the array in canonical order. When a project's `trigger()` is true and it has remaining `uses`, it creates or retains an active offer (`main.js:870-919`). Its `cost()` controls whether the player can buy it. A successful player purchase charges the stated cost and applies the effect; visibility alone never applies the effect.

### SYS-PROJECT-OFFER-LATCH — trigger at offer creation, affordability at purchase

The `trigger()` and remaining-use checks determine whether an offer first becomes available and whether a repeatable can be offered again. Once an offer is active, later updates do **not** continuously re-evaluate its entry trigger; they check its current affordability. An already displayed offer can remain purchasable after its original trigger becomes false until its purchase/effect removes it or a specific transition explicitly cancels it (`main.js:870-919`). Beg for More Wire (PRJ-002) and the goodwill offers are concrete examples; Release explicitly cancels certain offers and is not a general cancellation rule.

**PROPOSED:** preserve this source-exposed offer continuity as a gameplay lifecycle rule, not as a requirement to reproduce DOM nodes or an `activeProjects` array. The server must validate current affordability and purchase validity at command time. A replacement rule that continuously applies only `trigger() && cost()` would silently remove displayed offers and needs explicit product review rather than being presented as a generic cleanup. This is separate from repeatability: a replenished use still requires the project's display trigger to offer it again.

Ordinary Operations prices test aggregate `operations` but deduct `standardOps`; quantum output can temporarily reside in `tempOps` (`main.js:3413-3450`). Processors regenerate Operations up to `memory×1000`. After PRJ-003, remaining at that cap generates Creativity at a processor-dependent rate (`main.js:3266-3293`, `main.js:4319-4323`). Yomi comes from Strategic Modeling tournaments (PRJ-021), not from Operations or Creativity. These shared rules explain project affordability without making temporary-op rounding tails part of acceptance.

### 3.3 Completion, repetition, and persistence

A normal one-shot records completion and does not replenish its use. A repeatable project removes the consumed offer, replenishes its use as described in its row, and can be offered again while its trigger holds. Repeatability therefore depends on each project's own predicate and limits: PRJ-047 stops after ten chips, PRJ-044 stops at Trust 100, and the other repeatables retain the conditions listed below.

Completion state, remaining repeat allowances, dynamic repeat prices, and gameplay-relevant persistent bonuses must survive the implementation's own save/load model. Array-index save encoding, active-button reconstruction, `localStorage`, page reloads, audio loading, and button blinking are browser mechanisms rather than separate gameplay features. PRJ-095 is one-shot during uninterrupted play even though the official page refreshes its use count on load.

## 4. Deterministic purchases and stochastic dependencies

Every project's direct cost and effect is deterministic given current state. Randomness belongs to downstream systems and must not be attributed to the wrong project:

- Dynamic wire-price evolution affects PRJ-011 and PRJ-027.
- The stochastic stock market is unlocked by PRJ-022. PRJ-032 through PRJ-035 modify subsequent stock outcomes; PRJ-037 and PRJ-038 depend on the resulting portfolio/funds economy. PRJ-028 is a deterministic marketing upgrade, not a stock-randomness project.
- Tournament payoff generation and strategy outcomes affect Yomi associated with PRJ-021, PRJ-048 through PRJ-054, PRJ-061, PRJ-062, and PRJ-068. Each listed strategy's move rule remains deterministic for the generated grid and history.
- Battles and casualties are stochastic where PRJ-063, PRJ-064, PRJ-071, and PRJ-074 interact with combat. Random battle names also supply PRJ-073's dynamic title, but its costs and Honor reward are deterministic. PRJ-072 is a direct deterministic Honor conversion.
- PRJ-069's ambient hazards use deterministic accumulation with fractional-loss carry; they are not combat RNG. PRJ-070 changes swarm reboot status, not battle randomness.
- PRJ-046 and PRJ-047 are not random: chip values are fixed sinusoids, and their signed Operations sum is deterministic. Exact random streams and per-tick floating-point trajectories are outside fidelity acceptance; the gameplay-significant rules and distributions remain owned by their respective system inventories.

Project messages, price tags, the RevTracker readout, strategy and battle reports, chip state, Honor/Yomi readouts, and AutoTourney status are meaningful player feedback. Audio playback and DOM animation are feedback plumbing, not additional project effects.

## 5. Dependency chains and shared interactions

These are semantic dependencies for implementation and testing, not milestone groups.

### 5.1 Computing, Trust, production, and demand

- **Computing foundation:** global project availability supports PRJ-001; PRJ-001 → PRJ-004 → PRJ-005 uses `boostLvl`. PRJ-018 is a separate branch from PRJ-016. PRJ-003 unlocks Creativity generation and the Trust/idea projects, including PRJ-006, PRJ-013 through PRJ-017, and PRJ-020. PRJ-020 → PRJ-021 unlocks Yomi generation. PRJ-022 is a separate Trust-8 investment branch and supplies the practical portfolio system for PRJ-037 → PRJ-038.
- **AutoClipper stacking:** PRJ-001, PRJ-004, PRJ-005, and PRJ-018 add +0.25, +0.50, +0.75, and +5.0 to one multiplier, for 7.5 total when all are owned. They are additive rather than successive multipliers (`main.js:4303-4305`).
- **MegaClipper stacking:** PRJ-024, PRJ-025, and PRJ-026 add +0.25, +0.50, and +1.0 to their separate base multiplier, for 2.75 total. PRJ-023 unlocks that production branch.
- **Wire supply:** PRJ-007 through PRJ-010 normally multiply supply by 1.5, 1.75, 2, and 3 through supply thresholds. PRJ-011 is a parallel ×11 upgrade gated by stochastic wire price and may occur in a different order. Spool purchases and PRJ-002 rescue use the current supply (`main.js:722-731`).
- **Marketing and demand:** PRJ-012, PRJ-013, and PRJ-028 multiply `marketingEffectiveness` by 1.5, 2, and 5. PRJ-037 and PRJ-038 multiply the distinct `demandBoost` by 5 and 10. Both families feed demand (`main.js:4311-4316`). PRJ-014 → PRJ-012, while PRJ-015 → PRJ-013 → PRJ-028 → PRJ-029 → PRJ-030; New Slogan is not a formal prerequisite of Catchy Jingle.

### 5.2 Autonomy, terrestrial production, power, and space

PRJ-017 plus the post-human state normally produced by PRJ-030 unlocks PRJ-019. The chain continues PRJ-019 → PRJ-067 → PRJ-036 → both PRJ-040 and PRJ-041 → PRJ-042. Drone and factory counts then gate PRJ-055 through PRJ-060 and PRJ-066; PRJ-067 supplies the power system used by PRJ-065 and PRJ-045.

- PRJ-030 is the autonomy transition. It consumes the entire Trust pool, resets AutoClipper/MegaClipper counts, and can remove active PRJ-044 and PRJ-096 offers.
- PRJ-055 and PRJ-056 multiply factory rate by 100 and 1,000 and have no hard ordering relative to one another. PRJ-057's live production rule makes full output quadratic in factory count before power, not exponential in each added factory (`main.js:4273-4282`).
- PRJ-058 and PRJ-059 multiply both drone base rates by 100 and 1,000 without requiring each other. PRJ-060 sets `droneBoost=2`; harvesting and wire conversion each scale quadratically in that drone type's own count, not exponentially per added drone (`main.js:4105-4160`). These are observed live rules, not open interpretations.
- PRJ-045 requires post-human play and exhausted available matter, with stored power and unused-clip costs. It resets terrestrial facilities, returns all recorded factory/drone/farm/battery purchase outlays to unused clips—including `batteryBill`—and clears stored power through battery reboot. It opens space/probe play rather than merely adding another production upgrade.
- PRJ-070 does not unlock Swarm Computing. It only removes the in-space reboot status from a swarm already unlocked in practice by PRJ-066.

### 5.3 Strategy, quantum, combat, and rescue branches

- **Strategy:** PRJ-021 → PRJ-048 → PRJ-049 → PRJ-050 → PRJ-051 → PRJ-052 → PRJ-053 → PRJ-054. Seven additions plus initial RANDOM make eight strategies, gating PRJ-062 and the strategy portion of PRJ-068. PRJ-061 is a separate strategy-engine plus Trust-90 branch. Each PRJ-048 through PRJ-054 adds 1,000 ops to tournament price. PRJ-062 then fixes that price at 16,000 and doubles score-derived Yomi; PRJ-068's flat placement bonuses are separate additions, not the score-derived amount being doubled.
- **Quantum:** five processors unlock PRJ-046, which gates ten repeated PRJ-047 purchases. Signed quantum Operations can make PRJ-094 visible at Operations ≤−10,000. This is an optional restart path, not a prerequisite for space.
- **Combat and Honor:** one combat loss unlocks PRJ-071. Ten million combat losses unlock PRJ-064 and, together with PRJ-071, PRJ-063. PRJ-064 enables Honor progression and gates PRJ-072, PRJ-073, and PRJ-074. PRJ-069 hazard mitigation and PRJ-070 swarm reboot are separate space/drone branches.
- **Anti-softlock rescue:** PRJ-075 is a repeatable emergency Memory-to-clips conversion while the player has no probes and cannot afford one before the final Drift sequence. It is not an ending project.

### 5.4 Drift choice, persistent exits, and disassembly

At official in-game `milestoneFlag=15`, PRJ-076 opens the sequential PRJ-076 → PRJ-077 → … → PRJ-082 message chain. PRJ-082 offers the **mutually exclusive PRJ-083 Accept and PRJ-084 Reject** branches:

- **Accept (PRJ-083):** opens two alternative universe exits, PRJ-085 and PRJ-086. Choosing either resets into a new universe in that run. PRJ-085 adds persistent U prestige, where each level adds 10% of otherwise calculated demand; PRJ-086 adds persistent S prestige, where each level adds 10% of base Creativity generation speed. Both are additive per level rather than compounding ×1.1 multipliers.
- **Reject (PRJ-084):** stops new value-driven Drifter creation and starts the timed PRJ-087 → PRJ-088 → PRJ-089 → PRJ-090 → PRJ-091 → PRJ-092 → PRJ-093 disassembly sequence. It does not open either universe exit. Timer thresholds, staged resource returns, production shutdowns, and remaining-Operations pricing in these rows are gameplay requirements.

PRJ-094, PRJ-095, and PRJ-096 are independent special projects, not steps in the Drift choice or disassembly chain.

## 7. Gameplay acceptance boundaries

For each PRJ row, an implementation must be able to demonstrate the offer predicate, separate affordability check, charged resource, direct effect, completion/repeat behavior, and listed downstream unlock. Tests must also cover shared interactions: additive clipper upgrades, multiplicative demand upgrades, cumulative wire supply, dynamic repeat costs, ten-chip quantum limit, mutually exclusive Drift choices, both persistent prestige paths, and the complete Reject teardown.

All 96 array entries are covered as features. There are no project-array exclusions: no entry is defined but absent from `projects.push`, and no entry is source-supported as genuinely unused. Browser-only behavior is excluded by the product fidelity boundary rather than by misclassifying a live project as unused.

The official in-game `milestoneFlag` values cited above describe progression state in the source game. They are not repository delivery milestones and do not approve, freeze, or imply M1 scope.
